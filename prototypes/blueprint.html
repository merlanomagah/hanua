What I found

Pūtea already does the hard money work

I read your Pūtea repo before planning. It's an 11,500-line Mac app that already:

  • syncs 18 bank accounts from Akahu, and reads utility bills from three Gmail accounts
  • categorises transactions, spots subscriptions on its own, and tracks savings goals (your Belgium fund included)
  • runs a read-only local API on port 3456, and already reaches your iPhone through Tailscale

Recommendation: Hanua reads from Pūtea instead of rebuilding Akahu

Pūtea stays the money engine running in the background. Hanua becomes the place you look. That saves weeks of work and keeps one source of truth for your money.

Done: the duplicate money databases are archived

Budget, Subscriptions and Savings duplicated Pūtea, so they've moved into an Archive page inside Hanua in Notion. Nothing was deleted. A single Money book backed by Pūtea replaces them in phase 3.

Calendar

Getting your work day in without a sync

Your work calendar is Outlook. Notion Calendar has no API Hanua could read from, and Outlook won't sync. These are the realistic options, best first.

First thing to try: does your Outlook allow publishing?

In Outlook on the web: Settings → Calendar → Shared calendars → Publish a calendar. Choose your calendar and Can view titles and locations, then copy the ICS link. If the option is missing or greyed out, your employer has switched it off, and the screenshot option is the fallback.

Design language

Real objects that come alive

Your taste, in one line

Take something physical (a book, a shelf, a page, a ledger), make it feel real, then let it react to you as you scroll, hover and click. That's the style of Apple's product pages rather than its apps, and it's the direction for Hanua.

The rule that keeps it from getting tiring

You'll open Hanua twenty times a day, and motion that's thrilling on visit one is tiring by visit fifty. So moments of arrival and transition get the theatre (opening a book, the shelf filling in, the morning greeting). Moments of work (ticking a task, typing in ⌘K, reading numbers) are quick and quiet. No scroll-hijacking, ever. Reduced-motion settings are respected.

WhereWhat movesWhy it earns its place

  • Type: Apple's own system font for everything you read and tap, and your library serif for headings and moments that should feel personal.
  • Motion: spring physics everywhere (things overshoot slightly and settle, like real objects). Quick for work, slow and theatrical for arrivals.
  • Tools: CSS 3D for the objects, plus one small animation library for springs and scroll effects. No heavy 3D engine unless a prototype proves it's needed.
  • Colour: calm neutrals, with each life area keeping its bookbinding colour as an accent. Dark mode is designed properly, not inverted.
  • Feel: keyboard first on Mac (⌘K, arrow keys, Esc), and thumb friendly on iPhone, installed to your home screen.
  • Glance, then depth: each tile on Today answers one question at a glance. Tap it for the full book.

Roadmap

Build it in this order

Each phase is usable on its own. Use each one for a week or two before starting the next. Using it will teach you more than planning will.

  1. Foundations

    About one evening. Finish setup: Notion and Claude keys, connect the Hanua page. Turn Work into a Tasks database (with Work and Personal areas) and add a Learnings database. The money databases are already archived.

  2. Today + the one box

    The core. The Today screen with your tasks, learnings and Google Calendar. Ask and Feed merge into ⌘K, with confirm cards and a running cost note.

  3. Money

    The bridge to Pūtea. The money pulse on Today, a Money book that opens like the others, and Claude able to answer money questions from Pūtea's data.

  4. The work day

    Planning. Work meeting capture (link or screenshot), "plan my day" time blocks, and a two-minute end-of-day review that saves learnings to Notion.

  5. Everywhere, polished

    Phone and finish. Install to your iPhone home screen through Tailscale, the same way Pūtea works. Then motion, sound and haptics, and anything the first weeks of use showed was missing.
